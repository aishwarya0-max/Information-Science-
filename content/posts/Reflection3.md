Failure, was always something which was avoided or hidden due to it's association with it being a negative aspect. I tended to see it as something which was caused due to my own fault at a personal level. For example, while exams, I blamed myself for not performing well, beating myself for being incompetent. But the thing I ignored was the effort I have given for understanding the concept irrespective of the exam. This reshaping in thinking was due to this particular unit. This course, has significantly impacted me to push myself to develop  critical thinking to reduce errors but also develop ways to process them too. 

The book _Right Kind of Wrong_ and the case studies helped me to categorise and understand failure in a whole new way. It gave me a clear distinction of _basic, complex, intellectual_ failures which helped to to have strategised approach for solving **errors** I face. In the case study the situation an **_error_** became a **_failure_** due to ignorance of various warnings. 
The presentation approach helped because it helped us to move beyond surface-level explanation and started analysing the patterns, assumptions, and causes. 
What helped me particularly was connecting this to my own experience by trying to classify errors and evaluating of how it can prevented. For example, I missed the reflection blog submission deadline but instead of ignoring the error which was caused due to disorganisation, I created a todo list with active reminder to make sure it wouldn't repeated. Therefore, it comes down to how these ideologies are implemented into our life.
Overall, this course really helped by changing my perspective on failures or more precisely how I deal with them.
